# B1 Gold Lesson 4 — Teacher Quality Review

**Lesson:** Technology & Social Media  
**Level:** B1 Intermediate  
**Course Factory source architecture:** B2 Lesson 4  
**Review status:** PASS WITH REVISIONS — suitable for student preview, not production activation  
**Production activation:** false

## Evidence base

- Council of Europe CEFR global B1 descriptor: learners can produce simple connected text on familiar/personal topics and briefly give reasons and explanations for opinions and plans.
- Council of Europe CEFR spoken-language profile for B1: learners have enough language to communicate on familiar topics, can initiate/maintain simple conversation, and can link shorter elements into a connected linear sequence.
- British Council LearnEnglish labels Present Perfect Simple and Continuous as B1–B2 grammar and explains the continuous as focusing on an ongoing/recent activity or duration.

## Review findings

### 1. CEFR B1 fit — PASS WITH BOUNDARY

The lesson topic is familiar and personally relevant. The main productive demands are appropriate for B1: describe a changing digital habit, give a reason/example, make a practical suggestion, agree/disagree politely, and reach a small-group decision.

The target grammar can remain Present Perfect Continuous at B1 because it is used for familiar ongoing/recent activities. Learners are **not** expected to master fine-grained Present Perfect Simple vs Continuous contrast. The contrast is noticing/support only.

The lesson must not drift into the B2 Lesson 4 demand of evaluating broad societal claims, sustaining counterarguments, weighing evidence, or debating platform ethics. `algorithm` therefore remains stretch vocabulary and is not required for mastery.

### 2. Vocabulary — PASS

Six core items are manageable and functional: platform, privacy, notification, screen time, distraction, settings. Each is contextualised and reused productively. `algorithm` is appropriately scaffolded as optional stretch vocabulary.

### 3. Reading — PASS

The 193-word reading is long enough to provide connected B1 input while remaining concrete and chronological. It focuses on a familiar personal routine and practical changes rather than abstract argument. Questions are supported by the text.

Revision made: the multiple-choice distractors were strengthened so they are plausible near-misses instead of obviously unrelated answers.

### 4. Listening — PASS

The 75-second target is appropriate for a focused B1 listening task. The script uses a concrete workplace experiment and clear chronological development. The transcript remains locked until attempt 2, preserving the Golden Lesson architecture.

Revision made: multiple-choice distractors were replaced with contextually plausible alternatives.

### 5. Grammar — PASS WITH SUPPORT

The sequence moves from form and meaning to controlled practice and then communicative transfer. This is appropriate provided scoring rewards intelligible target use rather than native-like precision.

Revision made: implausible distractors such as `am check` were replaced with common learner-form confusions. The open final grammar item is formative rather than rigidly auto-scored.

### 6. Writing — PASS

The 80–120 word social post is a suitable B1 connected-text task. It asks the learner to describe a familiar habit, explain a change/result, and make a suggestion or ask a question. Visible success criteria and sentence builders provide appropriate support.

### 7. Fluency Mission — PASS WITH REVISION

The outcome—agree on three realistic digital-use rules—is genuinely communicative and requires negotiation. The group decision is now the completion gate. The lesson no longer requires learners to produce the Present Perfect Continuous a fixed number of times merely to 'pass' the mission; grammar use is collected as evidence for feedback instead.

### 8. Help Me Practice — PASS WITH B1 GUARDRAIL

The AI practice is topic-bound, hands-free after start, and based on short follow-up questions. It should remain on personal habits, practical benefits/problems, reasons, examples and suggestions. It must not drift into B2-style debate about algorithms, societal harms, source evidence or platform ethics.

### 9. Mastery and Boost — PASS

The 80% threshold is retained. If the learner falls below mastery, only the weakest-area Boost opens. This protects the Golden Lesson architecture and prevents unnecessary remediation.
