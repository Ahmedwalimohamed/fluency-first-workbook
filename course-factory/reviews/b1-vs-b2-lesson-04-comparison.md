# EnglishGate Course Factory — B1 Gold vs B2 Lesson 4

**Topic:** Technology & Social Media  
**Purpose:** Confirm that B1 Gold is the same EnglishGate product architecture at a lower CEFR demand, not a simplified copy of B2.  
**Production activation:** false

## Shared architecture

Both lessons preserve the EnglishGate Golden Lesson spine:

1. topic-bound warm-up / speaking entry;
2. contextualised technology vocabulary;
3. reading and listening on the same lesson theme;
4. Present Perfect Continuous as the target grammar family;
5. meaningful writing rather than isolated sentence production;
6. a final speaking/fluency task with an observable outcome;
7. adaptive evidence and targeted repair rather than broad remediation.

The Course Factory therefore clones **architecture and quality rules**, not source wording, texts, scripts, answers, or communicative outcomes.

## CEFR differentiation

| Dimension | B1 Gold Lesson 4 | Existing B2 Lesson 4 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main outcome | Describe changing technology habits, explain benefits/problems, give reasons, and negotiate practical screen-time rules. | Debate whether social media does more harm than good and reach a reasoned position after responding to opposing views. |
| Discourse demand | Personal/familiar experience, reasons, examples, practical suggestions, polite agreement/disagreement. | Qualified stance, concession, counterargument, evidence, synthesis, reasoned position. |
| Grammar | Present Perfect Continuous for familiar ongoing/recent activities; simple-vs-continuous contrast is supportive noticing, not an independent mastery target. | Present Perfect Continuous with explicit attention to duration, present result, ongoing activity, and result-vs-duration contrast. |
| Vocabulary | Core: platform, privacy, notification, screen time, distraction, settings. `algorithm` is optional stretch vocabulary. | Core: platform, privacy, algorithm, notification, screen time, distraction. |
| Reading | Concrete personal routine: Amina changes notifications, screen-time reminders, and study habits. | More abstract reflection: Yusuf distinguishes deliberate vs automatic technology use and rejects extreme claims about social media. |
| Listening | Practical workplace experiment: phone-free lunch, reactions, benefits, and a realistic rule. | Moderated mini-debate about attention spans, benefits of online communities, platform design, and balance. |
| Writing | 80–120 word social post about one digital habit the learner has been changing, one result, and one suggestion/question. | 60–80 word recommendation about a digital tool/platform including benefit, drawback, and recommendation. |
| Fluency task | Agree on three realistic digital-use rules for one week. The group decision is the completion gate. | Debate harm vs benefit, respond to opposing views, and reach a reasoned position. |
| AI practice boundary | Personal habits, practical benefits/problems, reasons, examples and realistic suggestions. Explicitly blocks drift into abstract platform ethics or sustained counterargument. | Supports balanced recommendation, stance, concession and more demanding follow-up. |

## Why B1 is not “B2 made easier”

The B1 lesson contains original reading, listening, prompts, question sets, distractors, writing task, and final mission. Its cognitive and discourse burden is deliberately different:

- **B1:** explain your own experience → give a reason/example → respond → negotiate a practical decision.
- **B2:** evaluate competing claims → qualify a position → respond to a counterargument → synthesise → defend a reasoned conclusion.

The topic and grammar family stay recognisably connected so the product feels coherent across levels, while the communicative work changes enough to represent a real CEFR progression.

## Course Factory decision

**Comparison result: PASS for preview.**

B1 Gold Lesson 4 demonstrates the intended Course Factory principle: **same EnglishGate architecture, original level-appropriate content, lower abstraction and discourse-management demand.**

This is not permission to bulk-generate B1 or activate production. The pilot remains preview-only until the complete QA pipeline and learner/teacher evidence are accepted.
